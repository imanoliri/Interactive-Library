# Valley of Dragons IV: Land of Chivalry

## Chapter 7: The Feast and the Conspiracy

The feast was held three nights after the western wells had been declared safe again.

Bellacourt had already begun to sound like itself.

The slow bells of warning had stopped. Market carts returned to their usual routes. The tournament resumed. Banners hung again from windows, and musicians played beneath the arcades near Côte de Lys.

The Chalice of Saint Armand had been carried back to Notre-Dame du Lys under guard.

Archbishop Anselm had not turned its return into a triumph for himself.

That impressed Alaric almost as much as the relic had.

The feast, however, was very much King Charles's kind of event.

The great hall of Côte de Lys was crowded with nobles, clergy, knights, merchants, foreign envoys and competitors from across the Bretonnian Lands. Long tables ran beneath painted shields. Musicians played from a gallery. Pages carried platters between benches while heralds announced late arrivals with more titles than Alaric thought any one person could possibly need.

Gaston leaned toward him.

"Do not look frightened."

"I have fought a god."

"Yes. But have you ever been seated between a bishop and a duchess who dislike one another?"

Alaric looked at the table.

"No."

"Then tonight is educational."

The feast was partly celebration and partly politics.

Charles moved from table to table, greeting men who governed their own lands, men who owed him allegiance, men who owed him nothing, and men who smiled as if friendship were merely another weapon kept polished for public occasions.

Yet there was warmth too.

A group of guildmasters from Bellacourt presented a silver basin for the restored western villages. Two abbots argued cheerfully about whose monastery brewed the better ale. Knights who had tried to knock one another unconscious in the lists that morning shared bread that evening.

Near the center of the hall, a minstrel sang the story of Saint Armand.

In the song, Armand crossed a flooded river to bring water to a besieged village.

Alaric glanced at Anselm.

"Did that really happen?"

The Archbishop kept his eyes on the singer.

"Probably not exactly like that."

"Then why sing it?"

"Because the truth of a life and the details of a song are not always the same thing."

Alaric considered this.

"That sounds dangerous."

"It can be."

Anselm finally looked at him.

"Which is why someone must remember both."

It was the longest conversation they had ever had without arguing.

Later, Alaric found Lord Raymond standing near one of the open galleries overlooking the inner court.

Raymond was younger than Charles, though not by much, and carried himself with less ceremony. He had spent most of the tournament laughing with knights, arguing with stablemasters and disappearing into places where an heir-presumptive was probably not supposed to be.

"You survived the Church," Raymond said.

"Barely."

"And the spiders?"

"Less dangerous."

Raymond laughed.

"Do not let Anselm hear you say that."

"He would probably agree."

Raymond stared at him.

"You really have changed things around here."

"I recovered a cup."

"You recovered a cup for a man who asked your dragons to leave the kingdom. That is different."

Before Alaric could answer, Count Bernier approached them.

Bernier had the smooth manner of a man who never hurried because he expected other people to make room for him.

"Lord Raymond," he said. "Sir Alaric."

Alaric still found it strange to hear the Bretonnian title attached to his name.

Bernier nodded toward the hall.

"A successful evening. His Majesty has a talent for making disagreement look like unity."

Raymond smiled without warmth.

"Sometimes looking like unity is the first step toward becoming it."

"Or the last step before discovering that it was never there."

Bernier raised his cup slightly.

"Forgive me. I am becoming philosophical."

He moved on.

Raymond watched him go.

"He hates Charles's project," Alaric said.

"He hates anything that makes another man more important than himself."

"Those are not the same thing."

"With Bernier, they often are."

Alaric had learned in the Valley that danger rarely announced itself with horns.

Sometimes it was a wrong footprint near a camp.

Sometimes a bird that should have been singing was silent.

Sometimes it was a man leaving a feast too early.

Bernier did exactly that.

Alaric noticed because he had spent enough time at Bretonnian ceremonies to understand that leaving before the king's final toast was almost an announcement in itself.

A few minutes later another man left by a different door.

Then a third.

None spoke to Bernier.

That was what made Alaric suspicious.

He found Gaston near the musicians.

"Who was the man in the dark green coat?"

Gaston followed his glance.

"Sir Aubert. Bernier's household."

"And the one with the red collar?"

"Steward to a minor lord from the northern marches. Why?"

"They are all leaving separately."

Gaston frowned.

"That may mean nothing."

"Yes."

Alaric started walking.

"That is why I am going to find out."

The corridors beyond the great hall were quieter.

Servants moved between kitchens and storerooms. Guards stood at stairways. Alaric passed through the gallery overlooking the kitchens and reached a narrow passage leading toward an older part of Côte de Lys.

There he heard voices.

Not enough to understand words.

Enough to hear urgency.

He moved closer.

A door stood slightly open.

Inside, Bernier spoke to three men.

"...before dawn. Once Raymond is outside Bellacourt, Charles has a choice."

Another voice answered.

"And if he refuses?"

"Then his heir remains unavailable when Lothar moves."

Alaric froze.

Lothar.

The name meant nothing to him yet.

The tone did.

A board creaked beneath his boot.

Silence.

Alaric stepped back just as the door opened.

Sir Aubert saw him.

For half a heartbeat neither moved.

Then Aubert drew a dagger.

Alaric caught his wrist, drove him into the wall and shouted for the guard.

The corridor exploded into motion.

Bernier did not try to fight.

He ran.

One conspirator overturned a lamp. Another slammed a side door behind him. Guards came from the stairwell while servants scattered.

Alaric chased Bernier through two chambers and into the inner court.

By the time he reached it, horses were already pounding through the lower gate.

Bernier was not among them.

Charles arrived moments later with Raymond, Gaston and several royal guards.

"What happened?"

Alaric pointed toward the gate.

"Bernier is moving against you. He spoke of Raymond. And someone named Lothar."

Charles's face hardened.

Raymond looked more angry than afraid.

"Where is Bernier?"

"Gone or hiding."

A captain ran into the court.

"Your Majesty. There has been fighting at the east service gate. Three men escaped."

"Close the city," Charles ordered.

The captain hesitated.

"All gates? During the tournament?"

"All gates."

The order spread through Côte de Lys.

Bells began ringing again.

Not the bells of sickness this time.

Alarm bells.

Within minutes, soldiers were moving toward the walls.

The feast broke apart.

Envoys demanded explanations. Nobles gathered their retainers. Priests tried to calm servants and pages. Somewhere in the hall, the musicians stopped playing halfway through a song.

Alaric found Raymond fastening on a sword belt.

"Stay inside the citadel," Alaric said.

Raymond looked offended.

"I am not a vase."

"No. You are exactly what Bernier intends to take."

"Which is why hiding makes me useful to him."

"Being alive and unkidnapped also makes you useful to Charles."

Raymond opened his mouth.

Then the outer horn sounded.

A royal guard burst through the archway.

"Lord Raymond. Your men at the eastern lodging request you immediately. One of your household has been wounded."

Raymond was already moving.

Alaric caught his arm.

"Wait."

The messenger looked confused.

"Sir?"

"Who sent you?"

"Captain Gérard."

Raymond pulled free.

"He commands my household guard."

That was enough for Raymond.

It was not enough for Alaric.

But before he could stop him, shouting erupted from the gatehouse.

The courtyard surged with soldiers.

For less than a minute, Alaric lost sight of Raymond.

When he saw the messenger again, the man was running.

Alone.

Alaric's stomach dropped.

He pushed through the crowd toward the eastern passage.

Raymond was gone.

## Chapter 8: The Rescue Mission

They found Raymond's sword in the stable yard.

Not broken.

Dropped.

Beside it lay one of his household guards with blood on his temple and no memory of anything after opening the side gate.

Charles stood over the sword in silence.

No one needed to say what had happened.

Bernier had failed to remain hidden inside Bellacourt.

So he had taken the next best thing with him.

"Close every road," Charles said.

"Already done, Your Majesty," replied the captain of the guard.

"Then search every wagon, chapel, manor and farm within ten leagues."

Alaric looked at the ground.

Four horses had left the stable yard at speed.

One carried more weight than the others.

The tracks turned east.

"They are not hiding him near the city," Alaric said.

Charles looked at him.

"How do you know?"

"Because Bernier spoke about taking Raymond outside Bellacourt before dawn. And because if he wants leverage, he needs somewhere defensible."

Gaston arrived carrying a bundle of hastily gathered maps.

"Bernier has estates north and east of the capital."

"He will not take Raymond to his own castle," Charles said. "Too obvious."

Alaric studied the map.

"Then somewhere he thinks you cannot reach quickly."

A royal scout pointed to a road running toward the wooded hills east of Bellacourt.

"There are old toll forts here. Most are abandoned. A few belong to minor lords."

"And beyond them?" Alaric asked.

"The marches."

Charles's jaw tightened.

"And roads toward men who do not answer to me."

Lothar.

No one said the name, but Alaric thought it.

The Dragon Riders were ready within minutes.

Without their dragons, they looked almost ordinary.

Almost.

They wore Bretonnian riding gear over their own armor where it fit, carried swords and short spears, and rode the fastest horses Charles could provide.

Master Bertrand watched them mount in the dark.

"You have spent a week learning not to disgrace yourselves on horseback," he said.

"Encouraging," Alaric replied.

"Now try not to die proving how much you learned."

The gates opened.

They rode east.

The pursuit was unlike flying.

On Vaelor, Alaric could cross forests, rivers and walls in a straight line. From the sky, roads were suggestions.

On horseback, every stream mattered.

Every muddy slope mattered.

Every bridge could decide whether they gained or lost an hour.

For the first time since arriving in Bretonnia, Alaric understood why so much power was built around roads, tolls, bridges and fortified crossings.

They followed the kidnappers through the eastern farms and into rougher country.

At dawn they found the first abandoned horse.

Foam covered its neck. One shoe was missing.

"They are pushing too hard," Gaston said.

"Good," Alaric answered. "So are we."

By midday they reached a fork where the tracks split.

Two horses continued east.

Two turned north.

A rider swore.

"Decoy."

Alaric dismounted.

He walked both tracks.

The eastern pair were clean and fast.

The northern tracks were deeper.

One horse had stumbled repeatedly.

"Raymond went north," he said.

"How can you tell?"

"The extra weight."

He pointed to the earth.

"And whoever is riding behind is not sitting willingly."

The riders turned north.

The land rose into wooded ridges.

Near sunset they saw smoke above the trees.

Gaston checked the map.

"There is an old watch fort ahead. It guarded the road before Charles's grandfather moved the border east."

"Who owns it?"

"Officially? A lord who has probably not visited it in twenty years."

"Perfect place to hide the heir to a kingdom."

They left the horses in a hollow and approached on foot.

The fort was little more than a stone tower joined to a low wall. One gate faced the road. A second, narrower postern opened toward the forest.

Alaric counted six guards outside.

"Bernier?" whispered one Rider.

"Not visible."

"Raymond?"

"Inside."

"How do you know?"

A shout came from the tower.

"Because that sounds like him."

Even through stone, Raymond's voice carried remarkably well.

"If you intend to ransom me, at least have the courtesy to explain your price!"

A guard shouted back for him to be quiet.

Raymond answered with something impolite.

Alaric smiled.

"He is fine."

The rescue plan was simple because complicated plans had too many opportunities to fail.

Two riders circled through the woods toward the postern.

Gaston and another rider remained with the horses.

Alaric approached the main gate openly.

The guards saw him halfway down the road.

"Stop!"

He stopped.

"I am Alaric of the Dragon Rider Order."

That produced exactly the reaction he wanted.

They knew the name.

They also knew there was no dragon behind him.

The captain stepped onto the wall.

"Turn around."

"You have Lord Raymond."

"We have no one."

From inside the tower came Raymond's voice.

"ALERIC!"

The captain closed his eyes briefly.

Alaric looked up at him.

"Very convincing."

The men on the wall shifted nervously.

They were not soldiers fighting for a cause.

They were retainers who had been told to hold a tower for money or loyalty and had now discovered that the most famous knight in the Valley was standing outside asking questions.

Alaric rested one hand on the pommel of his sword.

"Open the gate."

"You are alone."

"No."

The captain looked toward the trees.

That was the mistake.

Behind the fort, the postern burst inward.

The two Dragon Riders struck from inside.

Alaric ran for the gate as shouting erupted above him.

One guard lowered a spear.

Alaric knocked it aside, seized the shaft and pulled the man off balance. Another swung a sword. Alaric blocked, drove his shoulder into him and reached the gatehouse before the captain could organize a defense.

The fight lasted less than two minutes.

Most of the guards surrendered as soon as they realized men were already inside the wall.

Alaric climbed the tower stairs two at a time.

He found Raymond behind a barred door.

Raymond had somehow acquired a wooden stool and was holding it like a weapon.

"You took your time."

"I was admiring the architecture."

Alaric broke the bar loose.

Raymond stepped out.

"Bernier left hours ago."

Alaric's expression changed.

"Where?"

"East. He met riders here during the night. Not Bretonnian, I think. Different shields. Different accents."

"Lothar?"

"I heard the name once."

That was worse than Alaric had hoped.

Outside, one of the captured guards confirmed it.

Bernier had delivered messages and ridden east with several foreign-looking horsemen. Raymond had been left behind under guard.

"Why leave him?" Gaston asked.

Raymond answered before the prisoner could.

"Because I was never the final objective."

Alaric looked toward the eastern road.

"You were leverage."

"And a distraction."

The rescued heir mounted one of the spare horses.

For once, he was not joking.

They began the return to Bellacourt before sunset.

Halfway back, they passed a column of royal soldiers moving east.

The captain saluted Raymond and immediately turned his men around to escort him home.

Alaric rode beside Gaston.

"Bernier wanted Charles looking inward," he said.

"At conspirators, gates, loyalties, Raymond."

"While something else moved outside the kingdom."

Gaston nodded.

"Lothar."

Behind them, Raymond was explaining to a royal officer exactly how badly the tower guards had tied his hands.

Ahead, Bellacourt's towers were still invisible beyond the hills.

Alaric thought of Vaelor in the Valley.

For the first time since the dragons had left, he wished the distance between them were much shorter.
